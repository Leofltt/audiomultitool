What is an Online Signal Generator?

An online signal generator is a client-side audio utility designed to produce specific audio frequencies using four primary geometric waveforms (Sine, Square, Sawtooth, and Triangle) and three spectral noise colors (White, Pink, and Brown noise). It operates via the browser-native HTML5 Web Audio API, calculating audio waveforms locally without uploading any data to external servers.

Common Applications:

  • Auditory Testing: Mapping personal hearing range limits, including tracking frequency cutoff thresholds.
  • Equipment Calibration: Aligning input and output levels on mixers, DAWs, and hardware interfaces.
  • White & Pink Noise: Masking background distractions (White/Brown) or performing acoustic analyzer testing (Pink).

What is a Speaker Frequency Sweep?

A speaker frequency sweep plays a sliding audio pitch that shifts logarithmically between a chosen starting frequency (typically sub-bass at 20 Hz) and ending frequency (treble range up to 20,000 Hz). The sweep duration determines how fast the frequency changes.

How to Test Subwoofers & Speakers:

To test a subwoofer's bass response, set the sweep range from 20 Hz to 120 Hz. Watch for sudden volume drops or physical rattle sounds from the speaker casing, which helps diagnose physical structure defects or speaker cabinet vibrations.

How does the BPM Tap Tempo Calculator work?

Our BPM (Beats Per Minute) tapper measures the intervals between successive keystrokes or mouse clicks. It averages the last twelve tap intervals to display a stable and accurate tempo measurement. This manual counter is crucial for DJs, live musicians, and music producers trying to identify the tempo of an audio track without automated waveform detection tools.

Online Decibel Meter & Sound Level Calibrator

This sound pressure level (SPL) meter checks ambient noise using your browser microphone. It supports standard **A-weighting (dBA)** filters to model human hearing frequency responses, tracking minimum, maximum, and average values in real-time. This tool is perfect for home theater calibration, workspace noise assessment, and general auditory safety monitoring.

Understanding Decibel Level Safety Limits:

  • 30–45 dB: Very Quiet. Typical whisper, library environment, or quiet bedroom.
  • 50–65 dB: Moderate. Normal face-to-face conversation, quiet office, or rainfall.
  • 70–80 dB: Loud. Heavy city street traffic, vacuum cleaners, or alarm clocks.
  • 85+ dB: Hearing Hazard. Continuous exposure above 85 dB (e.g. lawnmowers, heavy machinery, loud concerts) requires ear protection.
